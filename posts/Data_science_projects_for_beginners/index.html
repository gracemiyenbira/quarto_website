Getting started with data science projects

If you are learning data science, you do not need complicated algorithms or huge datasets to begin building a portfolio. Start with a clear question, a manageable dataset, and an analysis you can explain.

Here are five beginner-friendly projects to try in 2026.

1. Student Performance Analysis

Explore how study time, attendance, and other factors relate to students’ academic performance.

Skills: Data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, visualisation.

Dataset: UCI Student Performance.

2. Personal Expense Analysis

Analyse spending by category and month to identify where money goes and which expenses take up the largest share.

Skills: Data cleaning, grouping, summary statistics, charts.

Dataset: Create a small spreadsheet of sample expenses or use a public financial dataset.

3. Weather Trend Analysis

Investigate temperature or rainfall patterns over time. Identify the hottest months, compare seasons, and visualise changes.

Skills: Time-series analysis, data visualisation, descriptive statistics.

Dataset: Open-Meteo historical weather data.

4. Wine Quality Prediction

Explore whether chemical measurements, such as acidity and alcohol content, are associated with wine quality.

Skills: Correlation, regression or classification, model evaluation.

Dataset: UCI Wine Quality.

5. A Simple Data Dashboard

Choose a small sales or survey dataset and create a dashboard showing important totals, comparisons, and trends.

Skills: Data analysis, visualisation, communicating insights.

Tools: Excel, Power BI, Tableau, or R.

How to make your project stand out

Do not stop at copying a tutorial. Write down a question, clean and explore the data, create meaningful charts, explain your findings, and document your code on GitHub.

Three well-finished projects that demonstrate your thinking are more useful than many unfinished projects.

The important thing is to start small, answer a real question, and build on what you learn. The goal is to demonstrate that you can turn data into understandable, useful insights.
